I'm proud to be a mentor/judge for [HackSR 2021](https://sites.google.com/simons-rock.edu/hacksr/ "HackSR"). If you're an alumni interested in cultivating startup ideation at Simon's Rock, please consider pledging up to $100 below.

The top X prizes (pending submissions) will receive a grand total of $500, the portions of which will be decided by a selected jury of HackSR volunteers. This $500 donation is covered evenly by the alumni who graciously pledge.

During the opening remarks, I communicated excerpts from the following message.

---

I'd like to thank everyone that helped bring us together, especially Iman and Samarth.

I'm Oscar, and it's a pleasure to meet you. I'd like to take 5 minutes to do three things: (1) tell you how I broke into tech (2) convey a crucial lesson, and (3) challenge you to put it into practice.

1. If you "seek to make the world *otherwise*" as Professor Asma Abbas put it, then your success is incumbent on your ability "to make". Outside of class, I learned to make by attending Hack Dartmouth with a group of my Simon's Rock peers invested in the success of our project and our team. I went on to attend and win prizes at two other hackathons, which helped me to land my first technical contract by showcasing our projects to a software freelancer at a local coworking space. He signed my proposal and sent over a 50% deposit -- without ever asking for a resume or code interview.
2. Like making a ~$10k web app, making the world *otherwise* requires more than making technology. It requires making other: observations, questions, connections, conversations, designs, teams, and especially customers that believe in your product so much they provide the resources you need realize its vision. These customers can be anyone: public agencies, private organizations, donors, or individual consumers just like us. So how do you gain the experience to make something good? Talk to people, empathize with their problems, and create a solution that's just good enough. Repeat and repeat.
3. Today, I encourage you to make something, anything at all, whether it's a stable project or a startup design with explosive potential.  To show that we the alumni support you taking intelligent action to make the world *otherwise*, we'll award the top projects with nominal cash prizes. No strings attached.

The jury will evaluate your project idea according to the merits of your hypothesis (problem, solution, insight) and the strength of your presentation in the areas outlined by YC's Kevin Hale -- see [How to Evaluate Startup Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DOtCl5PU8F0) and [How to Pitch Your Startup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=17XZGUX_9iM) -- below.

1. problem: popular, growing, urgent, expensive, mandatory, frequent. what is the problem? who is the customer? how many of the customers that you interviewed agree that this problem is really hurting them?
2. solution: don't start here. what are you making? can you feasibly make it? does the problem go away?
3. insight: competitive advantage related to growth, explanation.. why is now the perfect time to solve this problem, and why is your team exceptionally well suited to do so?
4. pitch & star quality. presentation and all other criteria. more details forthcoming.

--
