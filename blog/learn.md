## What does it take be a successful software engineer?

Knowledge, gained through a combination of education and experience.

## What do I need to know?

Generally, basic, foundational, and executive knowledge of theory, systems, and application.

Theory: Why things work

Systems: How they work

Application: Where they work 

Basics: Know what things are. 

Foundations: Explain them to some level of detail.

Executive: Make use.

It's important to know each of the three areas at each of the three levels.

## Specifically..?

Theory: Computer Science

Systems: Information Technology

Application: Business

Basics: In short, you need to know the basics of computer science to build solutions to problems your employers/clients are facing, and you need to know the basics of information technology to develop and deliver. 

Foundations: At a foundational level, this means evaluating the possibility and feasability of any computer program you may want to build, selecting potential computing platforms, and identifying the problems your clients are actually facing.

Execution: You should be able to design and implement your algorithms with appropriate data structures, integrating strategically with  existing products and services, while maintaining schedule, budget, and relationships.

## So how do I actually learn that stuff?

Execute that strategy all the way down.

Example: Now that we've established that you need to learn theory for software engineering, computer science in this case, we should figure out what to learn in computer science.

Here's what I suggest computer scientists should learn.

Level | Theory | Systems | Application
--- | ---
Basics | Formal Logic & Discrete Mathematics  | Analog & Digital Electronics | Algorithms & Data Structures
Foundations | Computability & Complexity | Computer Organization/Networking/Architecture | Programming Languages & Compilers
Execution | Statistics & Artificial Intelligence | Parallel/Concurrent/Distributed/Operating Systems | Software Engineering

Roughly, you should aim to ace any upper-level (senior undergraduate) course in computer architecture and in computer vision; those along with their long chains of prerequisites ought to cover most entries in the table above.

Software engineers should learn subsets of those fields based on what would reasonably be helpful, and waitlist the rest until it becomes useful.

## So how do I actually learn that stuff?

Sorry about that!

Take classes if you're in school; in particular, I would mostly recommend foundational theory courses.
Take classes online if you're not in school; I especially recommend Udacity (for basic knowledge) and Coursera courses which offer the full course for free.
That being said, my top recommendation is a class offered on edX: [The Computing Technology Inside Your Smartphone](https://www.edx.org/course/computing-technology-inside-smartphone-cornellx-engri1210x-0)

## Sounds like a lot, did you really do all that?

Yes, I took multiple courses in everything listed in the table above. My stack is a little different since I like engineering in general, robotics in particular.

Level | Theory | Systems | Application | Growth
--- | ---
Basics | Formal Sciences | Natural Sciences | Technology & Engineering | Arts & Humanities 
Foundations | Mathematics | Physics & Mechanical Engineering | Electrical Engineering & Computer Science | Social Sciences 
Execution | Statistics & AI | Robotics | Software | Management

Since I hold an associate's degree in liberal arts and a bachelor's in mathematics and computer science, I'm deeply experienced in theory and a lot of basics.
Since I am a full-stack engineer/contractor, I'm forced to jump in the deep end of execution; rather, I'm willing to do anything fun if it pays well enough.

Thus, I currently spend a lot of time learning about the theory and foundations of things in the systems and application columns.
For me, that means quantum gravity, network theory, control theory, computer vision, and computer architecture.

Primarily, I do robotics for a startup, research AI and data analysis as a social individual, and freelance as a well-rounded webdev/sysadmin/webmaster.
I'd like to supplement that by freelacing native mobile apps, DevOps, security, and especially embedded systems development.

Be good at what you do, and learn the things you wish you knew.

## Takeaways

Learn the basics, foundations, and execution of your field's theory, systems, and applications. Recurse? Recurse. Recurse!
