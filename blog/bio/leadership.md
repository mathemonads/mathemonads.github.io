My mother dropped out of college in Mexico when pregnant with my older brother, and moved us to the San Francisco Bay Area in northern California a few months after I was born. 
I've attended my fair share of broken schools, from an elementary schools whose faculty went on an extended strike to a middle school whose history teacher was arrested on-campus for immoral behavior and finally a high school whose math teacher was demoted mid-year for incompetence.
Aside from that, it's clear that I attended a very good public charter high school -- staffed largely by Teach for America corps members -- which boasts a 98% college enrollment rate and requires a minimum of 35 hours of community service per year to graduate.
I satisfied the requirement mostly as a subject tutor for 9th grade English and after-school tutor for the sciences.
Since then, I've been interested in academia, education, and technically-informed political advocacy.

As an undergraduate, I was proud to serve my class as the elected sophomore, junior, and senior representative. As a computer science major, I was also an avid fan of hackathons; I was particularly proud to participate in the one my campus sponsored, in which my team worked on a news aggregator which facilitated identifying primary sources for local events which received national attention.
I'm happy to report that we won the Most Effective Use of Technology award, and that I received the Rockstar Award for most helpful peer. 
After college, I kept learning about technology and participating in hackathons; since then, I also won the Civic Hack award at a hackathon for my team's app which presented an interface for quantifying the degree of sentiment in a given article (as a proxy for identifying bias). 
While I no longer actively hunt for hackathons, I remain open to social projects in need of technical talent. 

As a master's student, I've also had the privilege to serve my university as an elected representative for the past two years and to serve its affiliated Trust Fund Board. 
There, we've been able to allocate $100k toward renovating the campus bowling alley and $30k toward much need equipment for the Department of Theatre & Film. 
Allocating this money has also inspired me to raise money for causes I believe in.

For example, I found out last spring that my alma mater was hosting a hackathon event, much like the one I had participated in. 
Cognizant of what a great event it was, I decided to volunteer and help shift the focus from pure technology to social impact.
Once the event started, I was able to convince others this was a good idea and raise $500 from 17 alumni overnight to award as prize money the following day.
By all accounts, this was a smashing success https://simons-rock.edu/news/hacksr-2021-event.php

This reminded me of my first few attempts at fundraising.
My high school Science Bowl club needed money for equipment and travel funding, so I took it upon myself to bake cookies and sell them throughout the day.
I remember feeling proud the whole year until I met the other contestants; it became clear that the time we spent fundraising was just extra time they spent studying.
I viewed this as a great form of injustice and went home defeated.

I'm driven toward a career in teaching in part by the belief that kids deserve access to people and materials they need to learn.
