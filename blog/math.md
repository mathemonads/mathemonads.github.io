## Prerequisites
Basic experience with natural sciences, maturity with any formal science including some understanding of:
1. Informal and Formal Logic
2. Discrete Mathematics
3. Linear Algebra
4. Circuits

## Set Theory
*Def* Define sets/subsets/functions under the ZFC axioms.

*Def* Union, intersection, complements, products, cardinals, ordinals, Injective/surjective/bijective maps, relations, equivalence class, languages, free language

## Algebra
*Def* Homomorphism, Isomorphism, Semigroup, monoid, group, ring, polynomial, r-module, field, vector space

*Def* products, quotients

__Prove__ the positive integers are a semigroup, natural numbers are a monoid, integers are a group, rational numbers are a ring, rational polynomials of a single variable are an r-module, real numbers are a field, complex numbers are a vector space over the real numbers.

## Analysis
*Def* von Neuman ordinals, metric spaces, sequences, Cauchy sequences, open/closed neighborhoods, density, completion, compactness, real-valued real functions, continuity, differentiability, analytic/smooth functions, space of n-differentiable functions, Riemann integration, Darboux integration, integrability 

Construct the natural numbers from von Neuman ordinals, the integers from the natural numbers, the rational numbers from the integers.

Construct the real numbers as equivalence classes of limits of Cauchy sequences of rational numbers, and prove their are a complete, compact metric space.

__Prove__ real polynomials are dense in the space of continuous functions.

__Prove__ the product/quotient/chain rules, the intermediate/mean value theorems.

## Topology
*Def* open/closed sets, topological space, homeomorphism, discrete/indiscrete/metric topology, compactness, product/quotient topologies 

## Measure Theoretic Probability
*Def* sigma-algebras, Measure, Counting/Lebesgue measures, measure integration, Lebesgue integration 
__Prove__ the law of large numbers, Fubinis theorems, and the central limit theorem
